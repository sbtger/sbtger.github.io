Android Component Security

Peng Zhang·2020-07-20·中文

Communication between components

Android uses intents to pass actions, data, and extra parameters between components. Whether an external app can call a component and whether the caller is authorized to perform a particular operation are separate checks.

Four component types

ComponentRoleReview focus
ActivityHosts user interfaces and interactions.Verify identity and permissions at the point of use for personal data or account changes.
ServiceProvides background work and may accept bindings.Distinguish internal services from external interfaces and limit callable operations.
BroadcastReceiverReceives system or application broadcasts.Limit receipt and sender permissions for the use case. Handle empty or incomplete messages.
ContentProviderManages access to data through URIs.Check read, write, and temporary grants separately for the specific resource.

Explicit and implicit intents

An explicit intent specifies the target component. An implicit intent lets Android match a recipient based on the action, category, and data. When the recipient is known, narrowing the target helps keep identifiers and private parameters within their intended scope.

Dynamic receiver lifecycle

Unregister a receiver at the corresponding lifecycle boundary. A receiver that outlives its screen can retain a reference to a closed UI or continue to receive events after they are needed.
