Notes on Cloud GPU Servers

Peng Zhang·2020-03-15·中文

Why I tried cloud GPUs

I wanted to run deep-learning code, and my laptop's GPU was slow and had little memory. These notes describe services I tried and reflect the hardware, pricing, and terms at the time. PyCharm Professional supported running programs against a remote interpreter, which the free edition did not offer in my setup.

Tencent Cloud

I first tried Tencent for the reliability of a large provider. It worked well but cost more than the alternatives. Its COS object storage offered 50 GB for six months for RMB 1 in a promotion, and scripted uploads and downloads were much faster than my home connection.

Tencent Cloud

MistGPU

New users received an RMB 8 voucher. At the time a GTX 1080 Ti cost RMB 3/hour and an RTX 2080 Ti cost RMB 4/hour. Local uploads were slow. A Python script downloading datasets from Tencent COS to the machine was much faster. The server's IP appeared to belong to Alibaba Cloud.

MistGPU

Colab

Google Colab ran Jupyter notebooks in the cloud without setup. The GPU had changed from a K80 to a T4, although the free tier felt slower than I expected. I had trouble accessing Google Drive, and uploading files above 1 GB was particularly slow. Small training jobs worked well. Colab Pro was advertised at USD 9.99/month with access to T4 and P100 resources and possible TPU priority. I encountered a USD credit-card requirement for the subscription.

Colab

My preferred combination was Tencent COS and MistGPU. The promoted storage price, compute cost, and hardware made it attractive. I also found daily GPU rentals on Taobao but did not try them.
